What’s in a Bag of Chips? 1,785 Ingredient Lists Compared

Last checked 16 November 2026

MSG, yeast extract, sugar and frying oil in the United States, United Kingdom, Netherlands and Mexico, from the labels themselves.

We read the ingredient lists of 1,785 potato chips and crisps recorded in Open Food Facts for the United States, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands and Mexico, and counted how often each one names MSG, yeast extract, sugar and each frying oil. These are counts of products listed in an open, volunteer-built database, not sales. The method and its limits are set out below, and the figures can be downloaded.

What We Found

  • British crisps use yeast extract, not MSG. 44.4% of UK crisps list yeast extract (274 of 617), while 6.0% list MSG (37 of 617).
  • MSG is more common in American chips. 15.1% of US chips list MSG (145 of 959), about 2.5 times the UK share.
  • In Mexico, nearly half list MSG: 47.2% (25 of 53). Mexico’s sample is small, so the true share could be anywhere from about 34.4% to 60.3%.
  • Cottonseed oil is an American habit. It appears in 21.1% of US ingredient lists (202 of 959) and in none of the 617 British or 170 Dutch ones.
  • Sugar is common everywhere. Sugar or dextrose appears in 64.5% of UK, 61.8% of Dutch, 51.7% of US and 45.3% of Mexican chips. Palm oil is rare: 4.1% or less in every country.

How We Counted

  1. Data. The Open Food Facts nightly export of 16 November 2026, filtered to potato crisps that have an ingredient list. The same records power our chip database.
  2. Countries. A product counts in every study country Open Food Facts tags it with. We left out France: most of its records are British supermarket crisps also sold there. Other countries had too few records.
  3. Matching. We searched each list as printed, in English, Dutch, Spanish, French and German, for terms such as “monosodium glutamate”, “E621”, “glutamato monosódico” and “gistextract”, including common typos on scanned labels. Claims such as “no added MSG” were removed first, so they do not count as MSG.
  4. Ingredient count. Ingredients separated by commas, with sub-ingredients in brackets folded into their parent. It is a rough guide, not an exact count.
  5. Uncertainty. The CSV gives a 95% confidence interval for every share. For example, UK MSG is 6.0% (4.4–8.2%) and Mexico MSG is 47.2% (34.4–60.3%).

What This Can’t Tell You

  • Open Food Facts is built by volunteers who scan packs. It covers some brands and shops better than others, and it is not weighted by sales: a best-seller counts the same as a niche bag.
  • Some products appear more than once (different sizes or barcodes), and some ingredient lists are old or contain scanning errors.
  • Yeast extract contains naturally occurring glutamate. This study counts what the label names, not what the food contains.
  • Recipes change. Check the pack.

FAQs

Do British crisps contain MSG?

Few list it. In our sample, 6.0% of UK crisps (37 of 617) list MSG, while 44.4% list yeast extract, which is often used for a similar savoury taste.

Do American chips have more MSG than British crisps?

In our sample, yes: 15.1% of US chips list MSG, against 6.0% of UK crisps. These are counts of products listed in Open Food Facts, not sales.

Is palm oil common in potato chips?

Not in these four countries. Palm oil appears in 4.1% of US, 1.3% of UK, 1.2% of Dutch and 1.9% of Mexican ingredient lists in our sample.
