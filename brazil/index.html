Potato Chips in Brazil

South America

Brazil's chip story begins with two German immigrant families in Curitiba. In 1959 Elfriede Wagner and Maria Unger started selling home-baked pretzels door to door, and in 1962 their Padaria e Confeitaria Elma (El for Elfriede, Ma for Maria) launched Stiksy, crunchy salted sticks. In 1974 PepsiCo bought Elma and the São Paulo firm American Potato Chips and merged them into Elma Chips, still the name on PepsiCo's Brazilian snack bags, with the slogan "É impossível comer um só" (it's impossible to eat just one). Baconzitos, Cheetos and Fandangos followed, and Ruffles arrived in 1986. Today Ruffles and Lay's lead the potato-chip segment, alongside Yoki and Visconti, best known for batata palha (shoestring potato sticks), and Pringles, made in Brazil since 2019. Euromonitor figures quoted by UOL put potato-chip sales at R$3.7 billion in 2023, after average growth of 10.3% a year over five years, yet the average Brazilian spends only about US$4 a year on chips against roughly US$50 in the United States. Ruffles' Cebola & Salsa (onion and parsley) is a Brazil-only flavour, and Pringles now makes local flavours such as Churrasco, Cheddar & Bacon and pizza-inspired limited editions.

Top Potato Chip Brands in Brazil

PepsiCo leads Brazil's savoury snacks and, according to Euromonitor data quoted by UOL in 2024, owns all five of the category's biggest brands (Fandangos, Cheetos, Ruffles, Lay's and Torcida), all sold under the Elma Chips umbrella. In potato chips the top five are Ruffles and Lay's, Yoki and Visconti (strong in batata palha) and Kellanova's Pringles, which grew 34% in 2023 after moving production to Brazil.

Snacking Culture & Occasions

In Brazil, packaged chips compete with a strong savoury snack tradition: pão de queijo, coxinhas and esfihas are sold everywhere, and Euromonitor notes the country has about 400,000 bars. Ruffles and Lay's are bar and football snacks, while batata palha from Yoki and Visconti is a pantry staple sprinkled over hot dogs and stroganoff.

FAQ: Potato Chips in Brazil

What are the most popular potato chip brands in Brazil?

The leading potato chip brands in Brazil are Ruffles, Lay's, Pringles, Yoki and Visconti, with Ruffles at the top. They compete for shelf space in supermarkets, convenience stores and online, in a market estimated at R$3.7B (potato chips, 2023, Euromonitor). An average bag costs about R$14/bag.

What potato chip flavors are popular in Brazil?

The bestselling, most widely available flavors in Brazil are Original, Churrasco, Cebola & Salsa and Creme e Cebola. For something more distinctive, look for local specialities such as Cebola & Salsa (Onion & Parsley), Pizza Calabresa, Pizza Portuguesa and Cheddar & Bacon. Chips are typically eaten during bar & boteco petiscos, football on TV and batata palha on hot dogs and strogonoff.

What makes potato chips in Brazil unique?
