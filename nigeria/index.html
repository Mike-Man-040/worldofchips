Potato Chips in Nigeria

Africa

Nigeria's favourite crunchy snack isn't made from potatoes at all. Plantain chips, fried from ripe or unripe plantains, are a traditional snack alongside chin chin, kuli kuli and akara, sold everywhere from roadside stalls to supermarkets, and a 2023 WUSATA market report describes them as a local product competing head-on with potato crisps. Irish potatoes arrived in Nigeria in the late 19th century through missionaries, and the British colonial government encouraged growing them during the Second World War to feed troops in West Africa. Plateau State is the main producer today, but most packaged potato chips are imported. The same WUSATA report found that PepsiCo had no local manufacturing in Nigeria and that Lay's sold there at one of the highest prices it compared across African markets. Pringles' maker Kellogg (now Kellanova) bought half of Multipro, the Tolaram group's distribution company and Nigeria's largest distributor of packaged consumer goods, in 2015, and Multipro carries Pringles. Lagos shoppers buy chips at supermarkets and through delivery services such as Supermart.ng, where in November 2026 a 165 g can of Pringles cost ₦4,060 and a 150 g bag of Zifort unripe-plantain chips cost ₦1,970.

Top Potato Chip Brands in Nigeria

Imported brands dominate Nigeria's potato-chip shelf: Pringles, distributed by Multipro, plus Lay's and imports such as Walkers, Kettle and Master on online grocers. Local firms concentrate on plantain: Zifort, a Lagos-registered company, sells ripe and unripe plantain chips and sweet-potato chips, and Greenco, Delicious and Prime Way plantain chips sit alongside them.

Snacking Culture & Occasions

Plantain chips come ripe, which are sweeter, or unripe, often seasoned with onion or pepper, as in Zifort's unripe onion and Delicious' peppered packs. The WUSATA report notes that Nigerians tend to see potato chips as a more Western, salty snack and plantain chips as the traditional choice, and Lagos grocer Supermart.ng pitches plantain chips as a snack for parties and for sharing at the office.

FAQ: Potato Chips in Nigeria

What are the most popular potato chip brands in Nigeria?

The leading potato chip brands in Nigeria are Pringles, Lay's, Zifort, Greenco and Delicious, with Pringles at the top. They compete for shelf space in supermarkets, convenience stores and online. An average bag costs about ₦1,970/bag.

What potato chip flavors are popular in Nigeria?

The bestselling, most widely available flavors in Nigeria are Original, Sour Cream & Onion, Hot & Spicy and Peppered (plantain). For something more distinctive, look for local specialities such as Ripe plantain chips, Unripe plantain, onion flavour, Peppered plantain chips and Sweet potato chips. Chips are typically eaten during parties, office sharing and market snacking.
