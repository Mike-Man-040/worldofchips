Potato Chips in Italy

Europe

Italy claims the first potato chips of continental Europe. In 1936 Francesco Vitaloni and his wife Angela opened the Rosticceria San Carlo on Via Lecco in Milan, named after the nearby church of San Carlo al Lazzaretto. Its "patatine croccanti", thin slices fried and lightly salted, were sent out by bicycle to bakeries and bars. By 1940 the business had moved to Greco as "San Carlo... le patatine", and it now makes about 100 tonnes of chips a day. The market remains largely Italian and family-owned. Italy's competition authority lists San Carlo, Pata, Amica Chips, Preziosi Food and Crik Crok as the main producers, in a chip market estimated at about €580 million. Pata and Amica Chips are estimated at 29% and 24% of the market, and they also make most supermarket own-label chips. PepsiCo's Lay's is the main international challenger.

Top Potato Chip Brands in Italy

San Carlo, Milan's family-owned pioneer, is the best-known Italian chip brand. Pata (about 29% of the market) and Amica Chips (about 24%) are the other big domestic producers and the main makers of supermarket own-label chips, while Lay's is the leading international brand.

Snacking Culture & Occasions

In Italy, chips are a bar and aperitivo staple: San Carlo's first patatine were delivered to bakeries and bars, and bowls of chips still come with drinks. Supermarkets also stock a large own-label range made mostly by Pata and Amica Chips.

What are the most popular potato chip brands in Italy?

The leading potato chip brands in Italy are San Carlo, Pata, Amica Chips and Lay's, with San Carlo at the top. They compete for shelf space in supermarkets, convenience stores and online, in a market estimated at €580M (2024 est., per AGCM). An average bag costs about €2.30/bag.
