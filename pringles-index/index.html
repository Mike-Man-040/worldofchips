The Pringles Index (2026-10)

Last checked 27 October 2026

What one can of Pringles Original costs in 31 countries, in US dollars per 100 g.

Pringles are sold in almost the same can around the world, which makes them a handy, if crunchy, way to compare prices around the world. Like The Economist’s Big Mac Index, this is a light-hearted guide to price levels and exchange rates, not a precise measure. Every price below is linked to its source.

A can of Pringles costs 2.6× more in Turkey than in the US

Per 100 g, Pringles Original costs $3.96 in Turkey against a US baseline of $1.54 (a 147.4 g can at $2.27, Walmart). The cheapest markets in this edition are India and France at about $1.08. 31 markets, prices checked 2026-10-27.

Over- or Undervalued? (Big Mac Index style)

If 100 g of Pringles should cost the same everywhere, how far is each currency from that “Pringles parity”? Red bars mean Pringles are pricier than in the US at today’s exchange rate, which by the Big Mac logic makes the currency look overvalued. Green bars mean cheaper, so the currency looks undervalued. Taxes, import duties and local competition matter at least as much, so treat this as a conversation starter, not a currency forecast.

What Stands Out

  • Turkey: about 2.6× the US price per 100 g. Pringles are imported, and prices move fast with inflation.
  • Colombia ranks high largely because the peso has strengthened in 2026, a reminder of how much exchange rates drive any price index.
  • France and India are cheapest: France sells a 195 g can at a €1.89 regular price (taken from a promo leaflet’s “prix habituel”), and India’s figure is the legal maximum retail price.
  • United Kingdom: the regular price is £2.50, but the same can is £1.75 at Morrisons and Iceland and £1.00 at Poundland. Promotions matter.
  • Germany: the can shrank from 185 g to 165 g in 2024 at the same €2.49 (Verbraucherzentrale Hamburg).

Pringles are cheapest in France and India, but you don’t have to fly there for the flavours. Try an international chip variety pack at home.

Methodology

  1. One standard can of Pringles Original at its regular (non-promotional) retail price, including VAT/GST where prices are shown with tax. US prices exclude sales tax. We use one main source per country: usually a supermarket or online grocer, but some rows come from a price comparison site, a marketplace listing, a promo leaflet’s regular price or the legal maximum retail price (India). Each row names its source and price type.
  2. Local price ÷ can weight × 100 = local price per 100 g, converted to USD at the market rate.
  3. Index vs US = country USD/100 g ÷ US USD/100 g × 100.
  4. Implied PPP rate = the rate that would make 100 g cost the same as in the US; valuation = implied rate ÷ actual rate − 1.

One main source per country, one point in time: a snapshot, not a statistical average. FX: 2026-10-26 (ECB); COP 2026-10-26; RUB/EGP 2026-10-23; AED peg. * Low confidence: a range, marketplace, undated or possibly promotional price. These are shown but can be hidden with the toggle.

FAQs

Where are Pringles most expensive?

In this 2026-10 edition, Turkey: $3.96 per 100 g, about 2.6 times the US price of $1.54 per 100 g.

Where are Pringles cheapest?

India and France, at about $1.08 per 100 g. India’s figure is the legal maximum retail price, and France’s 195 g can sells at a €1.89 regular price.

Why compare per 100 g instead of per can?

Pringles cans differ by market, from about 102 g in parts of Asia to 165 g in most of Europe and 195 g in France, so comparing whole cans would be misleading.

How reliable is the Pringles Index?

It uses one main source per country at one point in time, a snapshot rather than an average. Low-confidence rows (ranges, marketplace or undated prices) are marked with an asterisk and can be hidden.
