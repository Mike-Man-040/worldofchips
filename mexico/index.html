Potato Chips in Mexico

North America

Mexico's $2.1B potato chip market is dominated by Sabritas (Frito-Lay's Mexican subsidiary) and Barcel, whose Takis brand has become a global spicy snack phenomenon. Per capita consumption is 1.1 kg, and the market grows at 4.5% annually. Mexican chip culture is inseparable from the country's broader street food (antojitos) tradition — chips are frequently doused in chamoy, tajín, and lime juice, transforming them from a simple snack into a complex flavor experience. Chile & Limón is the foundational flavor profile, appearing across virtually every brand. The Takis story is particularly remarkable: this rolled tortilla chip, invented in Mexico in 1999, has become one of the fastest-growing spicy snack brands globally, particularly among Gen Z consumers in the United States.

Snacking Culture & Occasions

In Mexico, potato chips are most commonly enjoyed during street food accompaniment. Other popular snacking occasions include antojitos & casual meals, fiestas & family gatherings, chamoy-covered as a botana. The average bag of chips costs $1.20/bag, making them an accessible everyday snack across all demographics.
