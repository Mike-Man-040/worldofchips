PepsiCo Trains Potato Harvester Drivers in VR to Cut Bruising

PepsiCo and German machinery maker GRIMME built a virtual-reality twin of a potato harvester to teach operators how to avoid bruising tubers, a costly source of waste for chip makers. In pilots in Brazil and Mexico, PepsiCo reports 87% more bruise-free potatoes and 1.7 times less severe bruising, though it hasn’t published methodology. The training is now rolling out in Romania and Poland.

Summer Drought Wipes Millions of Tonnes Off Europe’s Potato Crop

New analysis from the Energy and Climate Intelligence Unit estimates that this summer’s drought cut about 3.1 million tonnes from potato harvests in Germany, France, Belgium and the Netherlands, and more than 3.5 million tonnes once Great Britain is included. Combined with a smaller planted area, Europe faces one of its smallest crops on record, and processors are paying sharply more for potatoes than earlier in the year.

Sustainability

How Potato Scraps Become Perfectly Stackable Chips

Stackable chips like Pringles are made from dough rather than whole slices, which lets processors use undersized potatoes and the offcuts left over from fry production. As Tummers Food Processing Solutions explains, the scraps are pureed, drum-dried into flakes, milled into powder and mixed into a dough that is sheeted, stamped and fried, while potato water, peels and residual heat can be recovered too.
