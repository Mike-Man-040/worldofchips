Potato Chips in Germany

Europe

Germany's chip story has a military footnote: according to Lorenz, the first potato chips produced in the country came off Heinz Flessner's automated line in 1951 and went to American soldiers stationed there. The first German consumer brand followed in 1962, when Irmgard von Opel, inspired by a trip to the United States, opened a chip factory in Frankenthal with Carlo and Heinrich von Opel and named the brand Chio after their initials. In 1968 the Cologne sugar company Pfeifer & Langen launched Chipsfrisch in one flavour, "ungarisch", seasoned with Hungarian paprika. It became funny-frisch in 1972, and funny-frisch and Chio now both belong to Intersnack, formed in 1995. Bahlsen's snack business, today Lorenz, answered in 1992 with Crunchips. Paprika has never lost its grip: Germans bought about 95.7 million kg of potato chips in 2024 and nearly half were paprika, while salted chips, in second place, sold around 12.5 million kg (industry figures reported by Die Zeit). In a 2018 Splendid Research survey, almost 60% of chip eaters said they like paprika, and funny-frisch was the most-named favourite brand, ahead of Pringles and Chio. Potato chips are also the country's favourite savoury snack, chosen by 76% in a 2022 survey for the BDSI industry association, which says salty snacks are traditionally shared in the evening. Intersnack has described Germans snacking mostly at home in front of the TV, crime dramas and football, and says chip sales rise during football World Cups.
