Potato Chips in Australia

Oceania

Australia's chip history begins with Smith's. Frank Smith and George Ensor made the first Smith's crisps in Australia in 1931, in leased premises in Sydney's Surry Hills. They were cooked in 20 gas-fired pots, packed by hand, sold in threepenny packets 24 to a tin, and came with a twist of salt before pre-salting arrived. The company went into liquidation in the Depression and was re-formed in May 1932. In 1961 Smith's gave Australia its first flavoured chip: chicken. Since 1998 Smith's has been owned by PepsiCo, which merged its Frito-Lay business into it and had to sell brands such as Thins, CC's, Cheezels and Samboy. Those became Snack Brands Australia, now the main rival alongside Arnott's. Today's best-known chip brands include Smith's Crinkle Cut and Thinly Cut, Red Rock Deli, Thins and Kettle, and original, salt & vinegar and barbecue remain the staples.

FAQ: Potato Chips in Australia

What are the most popular potato chip brands in Australia?

The leading potato chip brands in Australia are Smith's, Red Rock Deli, Thins and Kettle, with Smith's at the top. They compete for shelf space in supermarkets, convenience stores and online, in a market estimated at A$1.56B (2020, per GlobalData). An average bag costs about A$5/bag.

What potato chip flavors are popular in Australia?

The bestselling, most widely available flavors in Australia are Original, Salt & Vinegar, Chicken and BBQ. For something more distinctive, look for local specialities such as Chicken, Honey Soy Chicken, Sweet Chilli & Sour Cream and Sea Salt & Balsamic Vinegar. Chips are typically eaten during beach & park picnics, bBQ & barbie gatherings and cricket & AFL viewing.

What makes potato chips in Australia unique?
