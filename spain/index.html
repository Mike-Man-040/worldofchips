Potato Chips in Spain

Europe

Many of Spain's best-loved potato chips (patatas fritas) were first made in churro shops. Bonilla a la Vista began as Salvador Bonilla's churrería in Ferrol in 1932. After the family moved to A Coruña in 1949, its chips, packed in 1 kg tins and delivered to bars by bicycle and later by motorbike, became a favourite of local hospitality. Production stopped in 1958 and restarted in 1988 at a factory in Arteixo, which still uses just potatoes, olive oil and sea salt. Patatas Torres also started as a churro shop, opened by Manuel Torres and his wife in Premià de Mar, near Barcelona, in 1969. In 2013 it launched what it calls the world's first potato chips with dehydrated black truffle, which have become its best-seller in the United States. In Priego de Córdoba, San Nicasio (founded in 1999) fries its chips in local extra-virgin olive oil and seasons them with Himalayan pink salt. The mass market belongs to PepsiCo, which entered Spain in 1971 by buying Matutano and its Burgos factory, the first factory PepsiCo acquired in Europe. The chips were renamed Lay's in 1998. Supermarket favourites include Lay's Campesinas, seasoned with paprika, onion, tomato and garlic, and ridged Ruffles Jamón. In Barcelona, chips are one of the three classic partners of the weekend vermouth, along with olives and cockles.

Top Potato Chip Brands in Spain

PepsiCo's Lay's (called Matutano until 1998) and Ruffles lead Spanish supermarket shelves. Family-made premium brands have built strong export followings: Bonilla a la Vista (A Coruña, since 1932) ships several containers a month to South Korea, Patatas Torres (Barcelona, since 1969) sells in more than 50 international markets, and San Nicasio (Priego de Córdoba, since 1999) is stocked by Harrods and Harvey Nichols in the UK.

Snacking Culture & Occasions

In Spain, chips are an aperitivo snack eaten before lunch. In Barcelona, "hacer el vermut" (having a pre-lunch vermouth on weekends and holidays) traditionally means olives, cockles in Espinaler sauce and potato chips, which often double as forks for scooping up the cockles. Upmarket olive-oil chips like Torres and Bonilla have also become a fixture of picnic hampers and gourmet food halls abroad.
