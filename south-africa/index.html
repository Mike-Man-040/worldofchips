Potato Chips in South Africa

Africa

South Africa's best-known chip grew out of a rusk business. In 1939 Elizabeth Ann Greyvensteyn, remembered as Ouma, began baking buttermilk rusks in Molteno in the Eastern Cape, and the family firm became Ouma Rusks. According to Simba's own history, her son Leon met Herman Lay, the American behind Lay's, in 1952, and in 1957 the family introduced Simba Chips, with Simba the lion as its mascot. The first flavours were Cheese & Onion, Tomato Sauce and Salt & Vinegar. Simba was sold to Fedfood in 1977, which became part of Foodcorp in 1992, and the company says it has been part of PepsiCo since 1995. Simba also makes NikNaks maize puffs, launched in 1972, and brought Lay's to South Africa in 1998. From 2013 it leaned into local tastes, starting with a limited-edition Steers Monkey Gland Sauce flavour and adding Mrs H.S. Ball's Chutney, Chilli Biltong and Smoked Beef. Its main local rival is Willards, owned by AVI, whose range includes Crinkle Cut chips, Flings and Big Korn Bites, while Cape Town's Messaris has hand-packed crisps since 1948. A 120 g bag of Simba Chutney was listed from R18.99 for grocery delivery in November 2026.

Top Potato Chip Brands in South Africa

Simba (PepsiCo) is South Africa's home-grown chip giant, with Simba chips, NikNaks, Lay's and Doritos in its range; the business moved to Isando, east of Johannesburg, a year after launch. Willards, part of AVI, competes with Crinkle Cut chips, Flanagan's kettle chips, Flings and Big Korn Bites, and Messaris has made crisps in Cape Town since 1948.

Snacking Culture & Occasions

Simba chips have been a lunchbox staple for generations, and South Africans are attached to their flavours: in Simba's 2021 "Choose me or lose me" campaign, Tomato was voted off the range while Mrs H.S. Ball's Chutney remains a favourite. Simba chips are sold at supermarkets such as Checkers and through grocery delivery apps.

FAQ: Potato Chips in South Africa

What are the most popular potato chip brands in South Africa?

The leading potato chip brands in South Africa are Simba, Lay's, Willards and Messaris, with Simba at the top. They compete for shelf space in supermarkets, convenience stores and online. An average bag costs about R19/bag.

What potato chip flavors are popular in South Africa?

The bestselling, most widely available flavors in South Africa are Salt & Vinegar, Cheese & Onion, Mrs H.S. Ball's Chutney and Tomato Sauce. For something more distinctive, look for local specialities such as Mrs H.S. Ball's Chutney, Steers Monkey Gland Sauce (2013 limited edition), Chilli Biltong and Smoked Beef. Chips are typically eaten during braai accompaniment, rugby & cricket viewing and school lunch boxes.
