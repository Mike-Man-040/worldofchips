Potato Chips in the Netherlands

Europe

Industrial potato-chip production in the Netherlands began on 3 May 1958 in Broek op Langedijk (North Holland), when local potato growers looking for a new outlet for Langedijker tubers partnered with British crisp manufacturer Frank Smith to establish Smith Potato Crisps. Because Dutch shoppers stumbled over the English word "crisps", the company soon renamed the product Smiths Chips. Starting with 30 workers in 1958, the Broek op Langedijk plant grew to 700 employees by 1983 and by 1988 was frying 120 kg of potatoes a minute — with 18 trucks a day supplying the Benelux, France and Germany, and workers taking home a free bag of chips every Friday afternoon. Acquired by PepsiCo's Frito-Lay, the Smiths potato-chip range was rebranded in the Netherlands and Belgium as Lay's in January 2001 (though the Smiths name lives on for snacks like Bugles and Hamka's). Its classic Benelux rival is Croky, founded in 1966 in Westrozebeke, Belgium, and famous for its ridged Bolognese, Paprika and Naturel chips, alongside Intersnack's Chio. While Naturel and sweet-smoky Paprika remain the everyday Dutch staples, the country's signature modern chip came out of Lay's 2010 "Maak de Smaak" crowd-sourced contest: Patatje Joppie, modelled on the tangy yellow curry-onion mayo served at Dutch frietkramen (fry shops), won the vote and became a permanent shelf staple in 2011.

Top Potato Chip Brands in the Netherlands

PepsiCo's Lay's — produced at the historic 1958 Smiths factory in Broek op Langedijk — leads the Dutch potato-chip shelf alongside Belgian-Dutch rival Croky (founded 1966), Intersnack's Chio and Pringles. Because the Netherlands is also a major market for tortilla chips (tortillachips / nachochips) for borrel platters and oven-baked nacho dishes, Dutch supermarkets (Albert Heijn, Jumbo, PLUS, Dirk) carry a wide tortilla-chip price ladder: Doritos leads branded tortilla chips (at Albert Heijn and PLUS, Doritos Naturel or Nacho Cheese typically runs €1.89–€2.59 for 140–170 g, around €11–€18/kg), followed by Croky Tortilla (Nacho Cheese and Paprika, around €1.79 for 160 g at Albert Heijn), Chio Tortillas and Tex-Mex brands Santa Maria and Cantina Mexicana, while private-label tortilla chips (AH, Jumbo, Dirk, PLUS and Lidl Snack Day — many produced in the Benelux by Roeselare-based Poco Loco) offer plain salted, nacho cheese and chili tortilla bags at roughly half the branded price per kilo.

Snacking Culture & Occasions

Dutch snacking revolves around gezelligheid and the late-afternoon or Friday borrel — drinks with friends, family or colleagues accompanied by bitterballen, cheese cubes, ridged paprika or Patatje Joppie potato chips, and bowls of tortilla chips with salsa, guacamole or melted cheddar from the oven. Chips are also central to Oranje football nights and King's Day (Koningsdag) on 27 April.
