Potato Chips in Argentina

South America

Argentina's industrial potato-chip market began in 1958, when Basque-Filipino entrepreneur Augusto Aboitiz founded Bun in Buenos Aires to replicate the U.S. potato-chip boom (soon adding Kesbun cheese snacks). In the 1960s Kellogg's entered with Frenchitas potato chips and Chizitos cheese puffs, while in 1964 the coastal resort city of Mar del Plata — located in the heart of Buenos Aires province's prime potato-growing belt — gave birth to Pehuamar. A decade later, the family-owned Argentine company 5 Hispanos S.A. launched Krachitos in the 1970s and brought the brand into supermarkets in the late 1980s. In the 1990s PepsiCo consolidated the category: it bought Bun in 1993, acquired Kellogg's local snack business (replacing Frenchitas with Lay's), and purchased Mar del Plata's Pehuamar in 1997. According to industry data reported by La Nación and El Cronista, PepsiCo (Lay's, Pehuamar, Doritos, Cheetos and Bun) accounts for roughly 80% of Argentina's salty snack sales, while 5 Hispanos' Krachitos holds 15% to 20% of the potato-chip segment as a fiercely loyal, value-priced domestic rival, joined by newer Argentine brands such as Quento, Gauchitas, Nuestros Sabores and Alwa. La Nación has reported that potato chips are Argentina's #1 salty snack ahead of peanuts (maní) and salted sticks (palitos salados), with five bags eaten across the country every second.

Snacking Culture & Occasions

In Argentina, potato chips are the cornerstone of the picada or copetín — a spread of papas fritas, palitos salados (fried corn or wheat sticks), maní (peanuts), salamín and cheese shared with vermouth, fernet con coca or beer while the fire is lit for a Sunday asado or before a football match (la previa).

  • Pre-asado picada & copetín with vermouth or fernet
  • Afternoon mate & park gatherings
  • Football viewing (previa del partido)
  • Weekend family lunches
