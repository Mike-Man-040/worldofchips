Potato Chips in Canada

North America

Canada has its own chip flavours that are hard to find anywhere else. Ketchup chips were probably invented in the 1970s by Hostess, then Canada's leading chip maker, which considered simulating the taste of fries with ketchup. Hostess merged with PepsiCo's Lay's in 1988, and Lay's ketchup chips remain a Canadian icon. All Dressed is often called the most Canadian flavour of all: a mix of barbecue, sour cream and onion, salt and vinegar and ketchup. Its earliest documented chip version comes from Quebec's Yum Yum, which claims a 1978 recipe. Old Dutch, founded in Minnesota in 1934, opened a plant in Winnipeg in 1954. It still runs its Canadian business from there, and in 2006 it bought Humpty Dumpty. Ketchup, All Dressed and Salt & Vinegar are among the Old Dutch flavours that may not be sold outside Canada.

FAQ: Potato Chips in Canada

What are the most popular potato chip brands in Canada?

The leading potato chip brands in Canada are Lay's, Old Dutch, Humpty Dumpty and Yum Yum, with Lay's at the top. They compete for shelf space in supermarkets, convenience stores and online. An average bag costs about $4.80/bag.

What potato chip flavors are popular in Canada?

The bestselling, most widely available flavors in Canada are Ketchup, All Dressed and Salt & Vinegar. For something more distinctive, look for local specialities such as Ketchup and All Dressed. Chips are typically eaten during hockey nights & sports viewing, cottage weekends & camping and everyday lunch snacking.
