Potato Chips in Colombia

South America

Colombia's chip aisle is a contest between a Bogotá-born brand now owned by PepsiCo and two family firms. Papas Margarita traces its roots to the 1950s, when, according to the brand's own story, Félix Asencio, a former Bogotá policeman, began frying chips by hand and selling about 20 cellophane bags a day at 10 centavos each. The company took the Margarita name in 1974, in honour of the wife of one of its founders, and Frito Lay bought it in 2000. PepsiCo says it is now Colombia's biggest buyer of industrial potatoes, at about 100,000 tonnes a year, and makes its snacks at plants in Funza and Guarne. Its main local rival, Comestibles Ricos, was founded in 1961 in Bogotá's Rionegro neighbourhood by Carlos Eduardo Silva and Raúl Suescún, and its Súper Ricas chicken-flavoured chips became a national favourite. In Cali, the Gilinski brothers founded Yupi in 1978; in 1995 it launched Rizadas, ridged chips. Chicken (pollo) and lime (limón) remain the classic Colombian flavours, and in November 2026 a 75 g bag of Margarita lime chips cost COP 4,990 at Éxito supermarkets.

Top Potato Chip Brands in Colombia

PepsiCo Alimentos Colombia sells Margarita chips alongside Doritos, Natuchips plantain chips and the De Todito mixed snack. Comestibles Ricos, still run by founder Carlos Eduardo Silva's family under his daughter Amanda Silva, makes Súper Ricas, Todo Rico and Super Crokantes, while Yupi (Rizadas, Yupis and Tosti) describes itself as the number two in Colombian snacks.

Snacking Culture & Occasions

Colombian chips are sold everywhere from supermarkets such as Éxito to the corner tienda de barrio; Comestibles Ricos built its distribution with red delivery tricycles and reaches more than 100,000 shops. Mixed bags are a Colombian speciality: Todo Rico, launched in 1984, combines potato, plantain and pork crackling (chicharrón), and its Criollo line (2007) adds papa criolla, arracacha and cassava.

Did You Know?

Colombia's first flavoured chips tasted of roast chicken: Comestibles Ricos says its Súper Ricas Papa Pollo, inspired by the rotisserie-chicken restaurants then fashionable in Bogotá, created the category in the 1960s, when the market only sold plain chips.

FAQ: Potato Chips in Colombia

What are the most popular potato chip brands in Colombia?

The leading potato chip brands in Colombia are Margarita, Súper Ricas, Yupi, De Todito and Natuchips, with Margarita at the top. They compete for shelf space in supermarkets, convenience stores and online. An average bag costs about COP 5,000/bag.

What potato chip flavors are popular in Colombia?

The bestselling, most widely available flavors in Colombia are Natural, Pollo (Chicken), Limón (Lime) and BBQ. For something more distinctive, look for local specialities such as Súper Ricas Pollo (chicken, since the 1960s), Todo Rico mix (potato, plantain & chicharrón), Todo Rico Criollo (papa criolla, arracacha & cassava) and Super Crokantes Gallina & Costilla de Cerdo. Chips are typically eaten during tienda de barrio snack, party accompaniment and football viewing.
